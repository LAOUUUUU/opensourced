Privacy policy

Last updated 12 November 2026

What Barely-free keeps about you, why, for how long, and who else sees it. Every line here is meant to be true of the code that runs the site today. If the code changes, this page changes first.

The short version

  • What we keep: your email address only as a scrambled fingerprint, your devices (the name you give each one, its public key and its address inside the VPN), and short-lived sign-in records. Our servers’ memory also holds each device’s last IP address and contact time, until about 5 minutes after it disconnects. We do not keep the sites you visit, the names you look up, or your traffic.
  • Who else sees some of it: Cloudflare, which carries every visit to this website; Quad9, which our servers ask for the names devices look up; Resend, which emails you your sign-in code and so sees your email address; the company that rents us our servers; and Ko-fi, only if you donate.
  • Why: to sign you in, to set up and run your devices, to stop abuse, and to keep the service running.
  • The risks: Cloudflare and Resend are American companies, and American law applies to what they hold. Our hosting company can see how much traffic goes through our servers. Anyone who can read your email can sign in as you.

1. Who is responsible

Laoel Beqiri, who runs Barely-free from Ontario, Canada, is responsible for your personal information. For any question or request about it, contact us. We answer within 30 days.

2. What we keep, why and for how long

A keyed fingerprint below is a scrambled value made from the original with a secret key. We can check whether an address someone gives us matches one, but we cannot turn an email fingerprint back into the address, so a stolen copy of the database would not give anyone a list of email addresses. A network-address fingerprint is weaker: there are few enough IPv4 addresses that anyone holding our key could work out which address made one. That is why those are kept for a day.

3. On our VPN servers

WireGuard, which our servers run, keeps in memory each device’s last IP address and port (so that replies can reach it), the time of its last contact, and how many bytes it has sent and received. Our servers make it forget all three about 5 minutes after the device’s last contact. That only happens once the device has disconnected: a connected device is in touch every couple of minutes. Open connections are tracked in memory while they last, as on any router. Each server also keeps the list of devices allowed to connect: their public keys and addresses inside the VPN.

None of this is written to a log; the servers’ logs record their own errors, not who connected or what they did. The website is sent each device’s last contact time and byte counts, and its last IP address with the last part removed. It keeps the contact time in its memory (section 2) and throws the address away.

4. What we never keep

  • The sites you visit, the names you look up (the servers’ own DNS keeps no record of them), or anything you send or receive.
  • A history of connections: when a device connected before its latest contact, or which device made which connection to which site.
  • Your email address in a form we can read, your name, or your birthday.
  • A device’s private key. It is made once, shown to you once, and never stored.
  • Analytics, adverts or tracking of any kind on this website.

6. Requests from police, courts and others

We hand over information only when a court order or warrant that is valid in Canada makes us, and we read each one carefully. A request from another country has to come through the Canadian authorities.

Even then, the most we could hand over is what sections 2 and 3 list, for the accounts the order names. We can find an account from its email address, from a device’s public key or its address inside the VPN, and from the IP address a device last connected from while a server still holds it. We cannot say which device made a given connection to a site, and we have no history of who connected when, because we do not record them. Every request we receive is counted on the transparency page.

We will tell the person concerned whenever the law allows. Because we do not keep email addresses we can read, we can only do that if they write to us, or on the transparency page.

7. Security and breaches

We keep as little as we can, store email addresses only as fingerprints, keep only scrambled copies of session secrets, encrypt the nightly backups, and send everything over encrypted connections.

If something goes wrong in a way that creates a real risk of significant harm to anyone, we will report it to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and say so on the front page. We cannot email you about it, because we do not keep your email address in a form we can read. We keep a record of every breach for two years.

8. Your rights

  • See what we hold. Most of it is on your account page. For the rest, contact us; we may ask you to show that the account is yours, and we answer within 30 days.
  • Correct it. Tell us if anything is wrong. To change a device, remove it and add it again.
  • Delete it. You can remove devices yourself on your account page, and delete your whole account at the bottom of it (“Delete my account”). That takes your devices off every server within seconds and deletes the account, its sign-in fingerprint, its sessions, and its devices and their kicks, at once. It cannot be undone. The nightly copies still hold it until they are replaced (about 30 days), and so does a copy made before an update of the website, until 5 newer updates replace it.
  • Withdraw your consent. Stop using Barely-free and delete your account.
  • Complain. Write to us first. If we do not sort it out, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

9. Children

Barely-free is for people 13 or older, and people under 18 need a parent’s or guardian’s OK (see the terms). We collect the same little from everyone: no names, no birthdays, no profiles and no advertising. If we learn that an account belongs to someone under 13, we delete it.

10. Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes and the change is listed at the bottom of this page. For a change that matters, we will say so on the front page at least 30 days before it applies. If we ever want to use your information for something new, or share it with someone new, we will ask you first.
