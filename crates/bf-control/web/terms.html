1. Who runs Barely-free

Barely-free is run by one person, Laoel Beqiri, in Ontario, Canada (“we” and “us” on these pages). It is a small independent project, not a company, and it is paid for by donations.

2. Who can use it

You must be 13 or older to use Barely-free. If you are under 18, a parent or guardian must agree to these terms for you, and should read the privacy policy with you. If we learn that an account belongs to someone under 13, we delete it.

You may not use Barely-free where the law forbids you to, or after we have removed you.

3. What you get

A free VPN: an account you sign in to with a code we email you, and a few devices (three to start with), each with its own setup for the WireGuard or AmneziaWG app. Your traffic goes through one of our servers, which blocks known trackers unless you switch that off for a device.

Barely-free is a small, free service and it has limits: how many devices an account may have, how many new accounts we take a day, and how much each server can carry. A server can be full or down. We may change these limits at any time.

A VPN hides what you do from your own network, and hides your address from the sites you visit. It does not make you anonymous, and it does not protect you from the sites you sign in to, from malware, or from what is on your own device.

4. The rules

Do not use Barely-free, or help anyone else use it, to:

  • break the law where you are, in Canada, or where our servers are (Canada and the United States);
  • attack, break into or overload other computers or networks, or scan them for weaknesses without their owner’s permission;
  • send spam or other unwanted bulk messages, or run open proxies or mail relays;
  • spread malware, run botnets, or commit fraud, phishing or scams;
  • harass, threaten or stalk anyone;
  • make, share or look for sexual material involving children, or anything else that exploits children;
  • share or download material that infringes copyright or other people’s rights;
  • attack, test or overload Barely-free itself (to report a weakness, see reporting a security problem);
  • use so much of a server that it harms everyone else on it.

The companies that host our servers have their own rules, and we must follow them too. If a host asks us to stop something, we will.

5. If the rules are broken

If we believe a device is being used against these rules, we can switch it off for a time or for good (a “kick”), remove devices, stop the account from adding new ones, or remove the account. Where the harm is serious we do this at once and without warning. We may also block a destination or a kind of traffic on a server for everyone.

If you think we got it wrong, contact us, say which device, and we will look again.

What we could be made to hand over, and what we cannot, is in the privacy policy.

6. Your account and devices

  • One account per person. Do not make extra accounts to get around a limit or a kick.
  • Do not sign up by script or any other automated means.
  • Do not sell, rent or share your device setups. Each one holds a private key: anyone who has it can use your device’s place on our servers.
  • Keep your email account safe. Whoever can read your email can sign in to your Barely-free account.
  • You can remove a device at any time on your account page, and ask us to delete your account (see your rights).

7. Donations

Donations through Ko-fi are gifts, not payment for the service. They buy no extra speed, no service level and no guarantee, and we do not refund them. Ko-fi’s own terms apply to the donation itself.

8. No guarantee

Barely-free is provided “as is” and “as available”. As far as the law allows, we make no promise that it will work, be fast, be secure or be available at any given time, that it suits what you want it for, or that any site or app will work through it.

As far as the law allows, we are not responsible for any loss or damage that comes from using Barely-free or from not being able to use it, including lost data, lost income, or anything someone else does. Where the law does not let us exclude it, our total responsibility to you is limited to C$100.

You are responsible for what you do through Barely-free. If someone makes a claim against us because you used it against these terms, you agree to cover the reasonable costs that causes us, as far as the law allows.

Nothing in these terms takes away a right that the law where you live gives you and does not allow to be given up.

10. Changing or ending the service

We can change Barely-free, or stop it, at any time. If the money for the servers runs low or runs out, new sign-ups stop first, and we will say so on the front page before any device is switched off. If we decide to shut Barely-free down, we will say so there at least 30 days before, whenever we can.

You can stop using Barely-free at any time.

For a change that matters (a new rule, or less of something we promise), we will say so on the front page at least 30 days before it applies, unless the law or stopping abuse needs it sooner. If you keep using Barely-free after a change applies, the new terms apply to you.

If a court finds that part of these terms cannot be enforced, the rest still applies.

13. Contact

How to reach us is on the help page. To report misuse, see reporting abuse; for a weakness in Barely-free, see reporting a security problem.
